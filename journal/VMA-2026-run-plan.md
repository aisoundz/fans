# Game Night Zero — the 2026 MTV VMAs

**Sunday 27 September 2026, 7:30–9:30pm ET / 4:30–6:30pm PT.**
CBS, simulcast on MTV, streaming on Paramount+. Live from Los Angeles.

This is the first FANS GAMETIME room that will have strangers in it.

---

## Why this one, and why first

**It needs nobody's permission.** Same architecture as the sports sibling, which
plays along with a live TV broadcast and has never asked the NBA for anything. No
venue, no artist, no tour manager, no production contact, no legal review.

**Resolution is free.** Every other room in this product needs a setlist
photographed at soundcheck or a production assistant tapping a panel. Here, who
won is announced on air, in front of everybody, and cannot be disputed. There is
no ops cost at all.

**On a broadcast the Gametimes are the commercial breaks** — the one moment the
entire audience already reaches for a phone. The sacred-set rule survives intact
and gets easier to keep: performances and award reveals are untouchable, and the
game lives in the ad breaks between them.

## The five Gametimes

| | Name | When | Worth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Red Carpet** | Pre-show, nothing decided yet — the sheet | 1 |
| 02 | **Cutaway** | First break, after the opener and the first award | 2 |
| 03 | **Right Back** | Mid-show, the longest break of the night | 3 |
| 04 | **The Envelope** | Final break — Video of the Year is next | 4 |
| 05 | **Credits** | After. Nobody has moved yet. | 5 |

Video of the Year is sealed at **Red Carpet** and settles at **The Envelope**,
ninety minutes later, live on air. That is the best-timed question available
anywhere on television and it costs nothing to run.

## What has to happen before the 27th

**Now — done.** The room is built and playable. Every question resolves either
from the award results or from something structurally true of every awards show,
because the performer lineup had not been announced when it was written.

**The week of (performers get announced ~10 days out).**
1. Fill the performer-specific slots — the "who opens the show" options and the
   mid-show performance questions. About six strings.
2. Re-check the nominee options against any late category changes.
3. Fill in the real run of show once the block order leaks or is published.

**The day before.**
4. Load the room and walk it once, end to end, on a phone.
5. Post the link. It is a free game, it needs no account, and the whole pitch is
   one line: *the VMAs, but you're playing.*

**On the night.**
6. Open Red Carpet at 6:30pm ET, an hour before air.
7. Advance the Gametimes manually against the actual broadcast — a human watching
   the show is the entire live-ops requirement.
8. Grade as the awards are announced. There is nothing to look up.

## What to measure — this is the point of the exercise

The reason to run this is not revenue. It is that **thirteen humans have ever
played STATS GAMETIME**, and this operation has never had an acquisition number
worth quoting. So measure exactly three things and nothing else:

1. **How many distinct humans took a seat.** Resolve uid to signup before quoting
   it — second devices and anonymous uids inflate a seat count, and that mistake
   has already been made once on the sports side.
2. **Round completion.** What share of people who started Red Carpet were still
   answering at Credits. A two-hour broadcast is a brutal retention test.
3. **Day-30 unaided recall.** Email the players on 27 October and ask them to
   name the winners and the performances without looking. Ask a matched group who
   watched but did not play. **If players recall more, that is the first evidence
   anywhere that a live event can be made more memorable by design** — and it is
   the number that makes the Live Nation meeting a different conversation.

## What NOT to claim afterwards

Whatever happens, one broadcast is one data point. If forty people play, that is
forty more than have ever played, and it is not product-market fit. Write the
number down honestly, including if it is small — the sports side's own history is
that quality climbed from 42% to 100% while the audience stayed at three, and
pretending otherwise would waste the whole exercise.
